# The rain that picks one person

## Intent

Some rains give the whole drip to one holder, which needs randomness nobody taking part chose — and that cannot exist in the same breath as the decision to hold a draw. So a draw locks the prize, waits, and is finished afterwards by anybody at all. That wait is the honest cost of the shape, and whoever picks it deserves to hear about it beforehand: somebody has to finish each draw, a draw left too long can only be unwound, and the randomness is good enough for a drip and wrong for a jackpot.

## Criteria

- **LOTTERY-1**  A rain can give the whole drip to one person instead of splitting it.
- **LOTTERY-2**  The winner is decided by something nobody taking part chose.
- **LOTTERY-3**  Nobody can join a draw after the thing that decides it is public.
  - **LOTTERY-3.a**  Somebody turned away while a draw is open is told to come back once it is settled.
- **LOTTERY-4**  Anyone can finish an open draw, not only whoever opened the rain.
- **LOTTERY-5**  Somebody looking at a rain with an open draw can see the next drop is waiting on a person rather than on the clock.
- **LOTTERY-6**  A draw nobody finished in time can be unstuck by anybody.
- **LOTTERY-7**  A draw that can no longer be finished gives its locked prize back to the pot rather than losing it.
- **LOTTERY-8**  Somebody looking at a stalled draw is offered the one action that frees it.
- **LOTTERY-9**  Somebody offered that action is told what it does before they send it.
- **LOTTERY-10**  Somebody choosing this shape is told every draw needs a second call before anybody is paid.
- **LOTTERY-11**  Somebody choosing this shape is told nobody sends that call on their behalf.
- **LOTTERY-12**  Somebody choosing this shape is told the randomness is good enough for a drip and wrong for a jackpot.
- **LOTTERY-13**  Somebody entering this shape is told it costs more to enter than the others, before they pay.
- **LOTTERY-14**  A prize held for an open draw is set aside from the pot rather than counted in it twice.
